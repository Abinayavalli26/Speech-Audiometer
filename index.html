Introduction

What is a speech audiometer?

A speech audiometer is an instrument that presents spoken words to a patient through headphones or loudspeakers and measures how well the patient hears and understands them.

Why is it needed?

Pure-tone audiometry shows how sensitive the ear is to single tones. It does not show whether a person can make sense of speech. Speech audiometry fills this gap by testing real-world listening.

Importance in hearing assessment

It helps the audiologist judge communication ability, decide on rehabilitation, and confirm whether a hearing problem lies in the ear's sensitivity or in the processing of speech.

Role in hearing-aid evaluation

Speech tests are used to check whether a hearing aid actually improves understanding of words, not just loudness of sound.

Signal Flow, Step by Step

  1. Input source: Speech comes from a live microphone or a recorded tape or CD.
  2. Input selection: The selector chooses the required speech source.
  3. Amplifier and attenuator: The speech signal is amplified and its level is adjusted in dB steps.
  4. Speech channel: The amplified speech goes to Channel 1 of the two-channel output.
  5. Noise generator: Masking noise is produced independently.
  6. Masking channel: The noise goes to Channel 2, with its own level control.
  7. Transducer: Each channel drives its own headphone or loudspeaker, so the patient hears speech and noise as separate signals.

  • Plays prerecorded speech
  • A good-quality recorder is required
  • A double-band tape recorder is preferred for two-channel units

C. CD Player

  • Plays recorded speech and word lists
  • Used for speech threshold and discrimination tests
  • An auxiliary channel can supply recorded masking

Two-Channel System

  • Speech signal and masking noise are supplied through separate channels.
  • The two channels can drive two headphones or two loudspeakers.
  • Loudspeakers are rated at 25 W each according to the reference material.
  • The noise generator supplies the masking noise.

Speech Audiometry Tests

A. Speech Reception Threshold (SRT)

Definition: SRT is the minimum hearing level at which a person correctly identifies 50% of the presented two-syllable words.

  • The two-syllable words are called SPONDEES.
  • Examples: Shotgun, Aeroplane, Workshop.
  • Each ear is tested separately.
  • The patient listens and repeats what is heard.
  • Can use live voice or recorded speech.
  • Threshold word lists are used.

The audiologist presents a spondee, the patient repeats it, and the level is adjusted until 50% are correct.

B. Speech Discrimination Score (SDS)

SDS measures how accurately a person understands spoken words, expressed as a percentage of correctly repeated words.

SDS = (Correct words / Total words) × 100

Why recorded speech is preferred

Each patient hears exactly the same words, at the same loudness and pronunciation. Results from different patients, clinics and visits can therefore be compared fairly. Live voice changes from one presentation to the next, so the result partly reflects the speaker rather than the patient.

08

Sound Cabin and Two-Room Arrangement

  • Live-voice testing should be done in a sound cabin.
  • The patient must not hear the audiologist's voice directly.
  • If both are in the same room, false results can occur, especially in people with normal or near-normal hearing.
  • In a two-room setup, the audiologist works in the equipment room and the patient is tested in the evaluation room.

10

Summary and Conclusion

A speech audiometer turns hearing testing into a test of communication. It shows whether a patient can understand words, not just detect sound, and it confirms whether a hearing aid truly helps speech understanding. Accurate results depend on controlled inputs, separate speech and masking channels, and proper sound isolation.
